# Contradiction Protocol

What to do when sources or lenses disagree. This is where the real insight hides.

## When Two Sources Disagree

### Step 1: Check the basics
- Are they talking about the same thing? (different geographies, time periods, definitions)
- Are they using the same data? (same dataset, different interpretation?)
- Is one source a higher tier than the other? (see [[source-evaluation]])

### Step 2: Find the root of disagreement
Usually one of:
- **Different data**: they're looking at different datasets → find out which is more comprehensive
- **Different interpretation**: same data, different conclusions → examine assumptions
- **Different scope**: one is global, one is country-specific → both might be right
- **Different timeframe**: short-term vs long-term trends can look opposite → document both
- **Different incentives**: one has reason to spin the data → check funding and affiliation

### Step 3: Document, don't resolve
In the deep-dive, write it as:
"Source A argues [X] based on [data]. Source B argues [Y] based on [data]. The disagreement stems from [root cause]. Under conditions [C1], A is likely correct; under conditions [C2], B is likely correct."

### Step 4: Upgrade to open questions if unresolvable
If after honest effort you can't determine who's right:
- This IS a finding. "We don't know whether X or Y" is valuable intelligence
- Add it to `open-questions.md` with the specific evidence needed to resolve it
- This often becomes the most interesting part of the research

## When Two Lenses Disagree

This is expected and healthy. Different lenses SHOULD produce tension.

- Document the tension explicitly in the deep-dive
- Ask: "What would need to be true for each lens to be correct?"
- The intersection of conditions is often where reality lives

## Confidence Adjustment
- 2 lenses agree, 1 disagrees → investigate the disagreeing lens deeper. It might see something others miss
- All lenses agree → be suspicious. You might have confirmation bias. Run [[contrarian]] again harder
- No lenses agree → your research question might be too broad. Narrow the scope
